## 4. What a Dubai apartment actually costs

[Costs as four stacked stat cards, not a table. Card layout: fee name top, pound figure as the large element, one-line note under it. Service charges as a four-row list with the £/sqft figure right-aligned and emphasised. Worked example stays as prose.]

**Section eyebrow:** The numbers first

**Section heading:** What a Dubai apartment actually costs

The advertised price is not the price you pay. Here is the full number, on a worked example of a £365,000 apartment, which is where our one-bedroom flats start.

### One-off purchase costs

[Stat cards:]

**£14,600** · Dubai Land Department transfer fee, 4% of the price
**£7,300** · Agency commission, 2%
**~£850** · Property registration and trustee fees
**£120** · Title deed issuance

[Small print under the cards:] Financing? Mortgage registration adds 0.25% of the loan.

Buying in cash, your total acquisition cost lands at roughly 6% to 8% on top of the purchase price. On resale purchases specifically, closing costs typically run 6.5% to 8.5%. Budget for it from the start.

### Annual holding costs

The cost that quietly eats your yield is the service charge, billed per square foot every year. Apartments carry higher service charges than villas, because you are sharing the cost of lifts, pools, lobbies and cooling. Across Dubai the median apartment service charge is around £3.45 per square foot, and the spread is wide:

[Emphasised-figure list, £/sqft right-aligned per row:]

- Mid-market towers such as JVC and Dubai Sports City · **£1.60 to £2.85 / sqft**
- Mainstream high-rise such as Business Bay and JLT · **£3.05 to £4.05 / sqft**
- Prime waterfront such as Dubai Marina and Downtown · **£3.65 to £5.70 / sqft**
- Branded and ultra-prime addresses · **£6.10 / sqft and above**

### Gross yield is not net yield

Worked example, using real Business Bay numbers. A 1,000 square foot one-bedroom at £223,000, renting at £15,800 a year, shows a 7.1% gross yield. Apply a £3.05 per square foot service charge and you lose £3,050 a year, and the yield drops to 5.7% before you account for maintenance, insurance or vacancy. A gross yield quoted at 8% is not 8% in the bank. On the call, we show you the net on the units you are actually considering.

## 7. One, two or three bedroom, which to buy

[Three cards, same pattern as the mistakes section. Title, two to three lines each.]

**Section eyebrow:** Matching the unit to the plan

**Section heading:** One, two or three bedroom, which to buy

Whether you call it an apartment or a flat, the right size depends on whether you are optimising for yield, for capital growth, or for your own use.

**Studios and one-bedroom flats**
The highest gross yields and the widest tenant pool, so they let and re-let fastest. Lowest entry price, lowest total service charge. This is where first-time buyers and yield-focused investors usually start.

**Two-bedroom**
Yield balanced against a larger, more stable tenant, often a family or a sharing professional pair. Rental demand is deep. The most common buy-to-let choice.

**Three and four-bedroom**
Yield traded for capital growth and scarcity. Fewer are built, so they hold value well, but they let more slowly and the service charge bill is larger.

If the plan is income, weight toward one and two-bed. If the plan is long-term growth, larger units in prime areas do more work. Tell us the plan on the form and we will match the unit to it.

## 9. What UK buyers get wrong

**Section eyebrow:** Before you shortlist

**Section heading:** What UK buyers get wrong

The mistakes that cost money are the same ones, again and again.

**01 · Reading gross yield as net**
The headline yield ignores service charges, which on an apartment can pull a 7% gross down toward 5% net. Always ask for the net. We quote it on every unit we show you.

**02 · Underestimating service charges**
They vary building to building, not just area to area. Two towers next door to each other can differ by £2 per square foot. We check the specific building before you commit.

**03 · Assuming off-plan is a discount**
In 2026, off-plan apartments often cost 10% to 18% more than a comparable ready unit. A payment plan is not automatically cheaper. We run the comparison for you.

**04 · Paying the asking price**
Asking prices in Dubai often sit 6% to 9% above the final sale price, especially on resale. There is room to negotiate, and we do it for you.

We tell you these things before you buy, not after. That is the point of using an advisor rather than a portal.

## 10. FAQ

[All four questions kept, rendered as accordions, collapsed by default. Copy unchanged.]

**Section eyebrow:** The questions, answered plainly

**Section heading:** Buying from the UK, answered

**Can UK citizens buy an apartment in Dubai?**
Yes. UK nationals can buy freehold apartments and flats in Dubai's designated freehold areas, with full ownership. No residency and no UAE visa is required to purchase.

**How much do I need to start?**
Across Dubai as a whole, entry budgets start lower, but for new-build stock in a well-traded community you should plan on £365,000 upward for a one-bedroom flat, before transaction costs. Our own projects start at that level. Budget another 6% to 8% on top for fees.

**Do I pay UK-style stamp duty?**
No. There is no annual property tax and no stamp duty in the UK sense. The main one-off cost is the 4% DLD transfer fee.

**How do I get paid the rent in the UK?**
Rental income can be collected and remitted to a UK account. We set up the management so it runs without you being in Dubai.
